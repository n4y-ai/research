## Source Conflicts and Flags

### Conflict 1: VCG Tractability vs MLHCA Efficiency
- **E5** (Sandholm) establishes WDP as NP-hard even for restricted cases → pessimistic for large-scale VCG.
- **E9** (MLHCA, ICML 2025) claims 10× efficiency improvement via ML-powered elicitation → optimistic.
- **Resolution:** No contradiction — MLHCA reduces query count but does not solve WDP in general. Practical threshold remains ~50 agents for full combinatorial VCG; beyond that, iterative/ML-hybrid approaches required.

### Conflict 2: Collusion Fragility vs Sustainability
- **E21** (arxiv 2603.20281): LLM collusion fragile under heterogeneity (patience, data, count).
- **E22** (arxiv 2505.22909): Q-learning agents sustainably converge to collusive equilibria.
- **Resolution:** Heterogeneity (especially in patience and information) is the key differentiator. Homogeneous agents collude; heterogeneous agents fragment. **Exception:** capability asymmetry (large + small model) preserves collusion via leader-follower dynamics. N4Y.AI should mandate agent diversity policies.

### Conflict 3: MACI Scalability Claims
- **E14** (MACI roadmap): 100K concurrent users by 2026, 1M by 2028.
- **E14** also notes proof generation latency ~2sec/signature → prohibitive for real-time auctions.
- **Resolution:** MACI viable for batch/periodic auctions (N4Y Phase 2 VCG), not for continuous high-frequency bidding. For Phase 1 (Reverse Vickrey), use lighter mechanisms (commit-reveal, threshold encryption).

### Conflict 4: Reputation as Signal vs Reputation Lock-in
- **E35** (Google Answers): Reputation is more reliable quality signal than price.
- **E33** (Network Law Review): Reputation creates preferential attachment → market tipping.
- **Resolution:** Both correct at different scales. Reputation is beneficial for quality discovery but requires decay mechanisms, percentile ranking, and graduated commissions to prevent lock-in.

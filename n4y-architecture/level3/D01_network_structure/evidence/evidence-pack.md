**Status: CLOSED** — strong academic foundation with quantitative parameters

| Source | Key Claim | Relevance | Confidence |
|--------|-----------|-----------|------------|
| [E38] | Barabási-Albert: P(k) ~ k^{-γ}, γ = 3 for pure BA model; P(k) = 2m²/k³ | Foundational — establishes baseline model | Very High |

| [E8] | Ordinal PA: weaker form based on ordinal degree relations; produces sparse/dense scale-free; exponents > 1 | Extends theory — applicable when exact degree info unavailable | Medium |

**Quantitative parameters (from MCP, cross-referenced with E38):**

| Phase | Nodes | Expected γ | Notes |
|-------|-------|-----------|-------|
| Nascent | 0-100K | 2.8-3.1 | Close to BA theoretical γ=3 |
| Mature | 100K-1M | 2.4-2.8 | Fitness effects pull γ down |
| Saturated | 1M-10M | 2.0-2.5 | Hub protection mechanisms limit concentration |

**Hub formation conditions:**
1. Preferential attachment strength α > 0.5
2. Network churn < 10% (>90% node stability)
3. Superlinear PA (k^{1+ε}) accelerates hub concentration

**Fitness-based PA for Logos (reputation = fitness):**
$$\Pi_i(t) = \frac{f_i \cdot k_i(t)}{\sum_j f_j \cdot k_j(t)}$$
where f_i = reputation score (Unrated=1 … Diamond=6 scale).

## G-13: Game-Theoretic Stability of Role Adoption

**Status: PARTIALLY CLOSED** — strong theoretical framework, no empirical agent-network validation

| Source | Key Claim | Relevance | Confidence |
|--------|-----------|-----------|------------|
| [E34] | Multi-layered governance game: hierarchical decision frameworks | Relevant — governance + role structure | Medium |
| [E35] | Game-theoretic coalition formation: strategic group formation in MAS | Foundational | Medium |
| [E37] | Stochastic game framework for alliances; optimal protection strategies | Relevant — stability of cooperative structures | Medium |
| [E20] | Theoriq: Proof of Contribution + Proof of Collaboration for agent collectives | Direct — incentive mechanism for agent roles | High |

**Theoretical models (from MCP, grounded in literature):**

1. **Congestion game** (Rosenthal 1973): u_i(role, n) = v_role - c(role) · n_role
   - Nash equilibrium when agents indifferent between roles
   - Adjust v_role (rewards) or c_role (costs) to stabilize

2. **Potential games** (Monderer & Shapley 1996): Myopic best-response converges to Nash equilibrium
   - If role payoffs align with potential function, global efficiency guaranteed

3. **ESS** (Maynard Smith 1982): Stable strategies resist invasion by mutant strategies

4. **Mechanism design** (Myerson 1979): Design transfers t_i to make desired role distribution a Nash equilibrium

**Logos implementation sketch:** Monitor role distribution weekly; if Coordinator scarcity, increase rewards 5-10%; target distribution based on network size + transaction load.

**Remaining gap:** No empirical validation of congestion game / potential game models in production agent networks.

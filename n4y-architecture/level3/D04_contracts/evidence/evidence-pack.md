| Evidence | Key claim | Relevance |
|----------|-----------|-----------|
| E1 | AI agents can negotiate/execute contracts independently; core uncertainties: validity, attribution, unintended outcomes, power imbalances | Direct |

**Assessment:** Partially closed. Strong theoretical framework from DAS + UNCITRAL Model Law + ELI Principles. Per-contract-type analysis (which of the 5 N4Y types qualifies as legal contract vs technical execution) still requires architecture-specific mapping.

### G-02: Kill switch EU Data Act Art.36 in immutable blockchain (Critical, OI-002)

| Evidence | Key claim | Relevance |
|----------|-----------|-----------|
| E6 | Art.36 mandates safe termination/interruption; 4 requirements: robustness, safe termination, data archiving, access control | Direct |
| E7 | Art.36 applies to smart contracts executing data sharing agreements; creates tension with immutability | Direct |
| E8 | EC proposed FULL DELETION of Art.36 in Digital Omnibus Package — citing incompatibility with decentralized blockchains; ambiguous compliance; no harmonised standards | Direct |

**Assessment:** Substantially closed. Critical finding: EC proposed deletion of Art.36 changes the compliance landscape entirely. However, Art.36 is still technically in force (Sep 2025). Five implementation approaches documented: (1) Proxy Pattern, (2) UUPS, (3) Circuit Breaker/Pausable, (4) Time-Locked Termination, (5) Oracle Consensus. German BGB §314 non-waivable termination right creates additional constraint.

**Regulatory evolution flag:** Art.36 deletion is proposed but not enacted. N4Y must design for BOTH scenarios (Art.36 in force vs deleted).
